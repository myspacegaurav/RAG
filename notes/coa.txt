Computer architecture refers to the design and organization of a computer’s fundamental components. At its core, it defines the interaction between the CPU, memory, and input/output devices. The CPU itself consists of the control unit, arithmetic logic unit (ALU), and registers, working together to execute instructions from programs. Instruction sets (like RISC and CISC) determine how operations are encoded and processed. Memory hierarchy plays a crucial role, with registers, cache, RAM, and secondary storage arranged to balance speed and cost. Concepts like pipelining, parallelism, and superscalar execution improve performance by overlapping instruction execution. Bus systems and interconnects facilitate communication between components, while addressing modes define how data is accessed. Modern architectures also incorporate multicore processors, GPUs for parallel computation, and specialized accelerators for tasks like AI.
